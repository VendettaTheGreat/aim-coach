## Four ground-tracking subcategories

- **Reading:** Takes established fundamentals into less predictable, more erratic bot movement and faster velocity changes. Stresses reading both speed and direction while maintaining tracking fundamentals.
- **Hybrid:** Still has varied/erratic speed, but is more readable and has less strafe variance than Reading. Stresses precise tracking while combining moderate reading and mouse-control demand; it is neither pure reading nor pure technique.
- **Technique:** Small-target precision plus fast direction changes. Stresses quick, controlled corrections and maintaining a precise speed match.
- **Fluidity:** Smoothly tracking through velocity changes with more strafe variance; discourages predicting and supports progression toward Hybrid/Reading.

These labels describe scenario demands, not exclusive biological abilities. A scenario may stress several categories; use the dominant task demand and verify the exact active profile.

## Difficulty bands and benchmark structure

- The sheets use **Entry**, **Easy**, and **Hard** scenario bands. The overview describes Entry as foundational, Easy as a later benchmark band, and Hard as advanced. These are not deterministic player-rank placements: the workbook itself notes overlap between levels and variance among players.
- Score columns labelled with ranks are benchmark score thresholds, not instructions to assign a player to that category from rank alone. Use observed control and the scenario's task demand to select a suitable entry point.
- The workbook has two Entry fundamentals: `Close Long Strafes Invincible` and `Ground Plaza Sparky V3 Invincible 5`. Its overview describes long-strafe work as learning to react to a direction change and continue tracking; the Ground Plaza fundamental adds variable depth and speed.
- The benchmark's listed Easy and Hard scenarios are examples for category/tier discovery—not automatic routine inclusions. Exact titles may be stale or have difficulty variants; check official listings before use.

## Practical mechanics from Technique Overview

The workbook describes a ground-reactive cycle: respond to a strafe change, reacquire, then match the bot's speed until the next change. The target's acceleration affects the correction: readable deceleration may permit a shorter adjustment or continuous tracking through a reversal; instant-acceleration bots offer less advance information and demand quicker reacquisition. Variable-depth targets change relative speed as they approach or recede. Very short, hard-to-read strafes can compound overcorrections if the player loses visual information while correcting; an intentionally more controlled correction can preserve the read before speed is increased again. Blink-like displacement adds a larger reacquisition demand followed by a smooth return to tracking.

**Adaptation for this user:** The sheet's own explanatory text includes a wrist-led flick with a tension spike/release and body-part routing. Treat that as the authors' technique framework, not a required prescription. ## Using the taxonomy to build a progressive routine

For a smooth-and-quick tracking goal, use the categories as a progression map rather than isolated labels: establish the relevant **Technique/Fluidity** control, increase pace while preserving tracking quality, then add **Hybrid** speed variation and finally **Reading** unpredictability/fast velocity changes. Overload one demand at a time (speed, acceleration, target size, strafe variance, depth, or player movement), and advance when observable form remains stable. The easiest band is a launch point, not a permanent speed cap; the goal is the fastest representative task the player can execute cleanly.

Keep target motion separate from player movement: these ground categories mainly describe the bot's motion/aim demand. Do not infer WASD/dodge from target strafe names. Add player movement as a separate progression step only when the exact scenario confirms it. Per user clarification, `OW` in the relevant ground scenario titles indicates Overwatch strafe physics—near-instant ground acceleration and sharp velocity reversal rather than a long accel/decel ramp. It does not mean player movement is enabled.

The workbook states bot overlay/override is disallowed for its scenarios except `Close Fast Strafes Invincible - Thin` and its Easy counterpart for benchmark-score balancing. Treat that as a benchmark scoring rule only; never infer a routine setting or a general scenario permission from it.
