### Motor-learning analysis — why it likely didn't transfer (hypotheses, stated as hypotheses)

1. **Internal attentional focus overload (strongest hypothesis).** The program's core method is conscious monitoring of body parts and pressure percentages (index 50%, thumb 60%, "feel your shoulder push," per-bot tension budgets). Motor-learning research (Wulf et al.) consistently shows internal focus (on body parts) increases muscle activation/co-contraction and degrades movement economy vs external focus (on target/effect). The user's own logs match: tension 5–8/10 with jitter exactly at tension-switch moments; his recurring self-correction in every doc is some form of "use LESS tension / relax the grip" — the win came from abandoning the prescription's active-tension layer, not from executing it.
2. **Reinvestment under pressure (Masters, conscious processing).** User self-reports tensing in high-pressure in-game situations and deliberately trained with score bar ON. A program built on explicit pressure-percentage rules gives the anxious in-game brain more explicit rules to reinvest in → choking pattern. The one intervention with reported immediate improvement (wrist on pad, ZERO downward force) is a postural constraint that REMOVES conscious pressure control — constraint-led, not instruction-led.
3. **Performance ≠ learning.** Nearly all score targets were hit in practice (many exceeded) yet in-game transfer failed after a month. Hitting targets while consciously managing tension demonstrates performance under explicit rules; it does not build the automatic target-driven control that in-game situations (Mercy GA arcs at speed, under pressure) demand.
4. **Volume/load.** 14 drills × 8–10 reps (session 1) and 40+ min/day multi-sens cycles (session 3) on top of games — high load while logged tension was already 5–8/10; consistent with inverted-U overload (practice quality degrading within sessions).
5. **What was RIGHT in Serf's method (keep these):** factor decomposition (size/speed/acceleration/strafe-length/time scale) = structured variable practice; 10–90% accuracy difficulty window = challenge-point framing; inside-edge/anti-over-aim rule; read-don't-predict; OW-specific instant-acceleration scenario engineering; humanoid-model in-game translation (shoulder→stomach fade); movement-led aim (keyboard does bulk correction); wrist-on-pad/zero-downward air baseline; practice-after-games + skip-if-fatigued rest rule.
